An emergency fund is money set aside to cover unexpected expenses, like car repairs, medical bills, or job loss. It’s the foundation of financial stability and should come before investing in riskier assets.

## Why an Emergency Fund Matters
- Prevents relying on **credit cards or loans** during emergencies.  
- Provides **peace of mind** knowing you can handle unexpected costs.  
- Reduces financial stress and helps you stay on track with long-term goals.  

---

## How Much to Save
- **Starter fund**: $500–$1,000 for basic emergencies.  
- **Full fund**: 3–6 months of essential expenses (rent, food, transportation, insurance).  
- Adjust based on:  
  - Job stability (gig work may require more).  
  - Dependents (families should aim for larger funds).  
  - Location and living costs.  

---

## Where to Keep It
- **High-Yield Savings Account (HYSA)**: Best balance of safety and growth.  
- **Regular Savings Account**: Fine if your bank doesn’t offer HYSA.  
- Avoid tying up emergency funds in:  
  - Stocks (too risky).  
  - Certificates of Deposit (penalties for early withdrawal).  
  - Cash at home (not safe, no interest).  

---

## How to Build One
- Save a small, regular amount each month—even $25 adds up.  
- Use windfalls (tax refunds, bonuses, gift money) to boost the fund.  
- Automate transfers into your savings account.  
- Start small and increase over time; don’t wait until you can save “the perfect amount.”  

---

## Quick Tips
- Label your account “Emergency Fund” so you’re less tempted to dip into it.  
- Keep it separate from your checking account.  
- Focus on this before heavy investing—it’s your financial safety net.
