Retirement accounts are tax-advantaged savings plans designed to help you build financial security for the future. Starting early makes a big difference thanks to compound growth, but even small contributions can add up over time.

## Why Retirement Accounts Matter
- Offer **tax advantages** that regular investment accounts don’t.  
- Encourage consistent, long-term saving.  
- Protect money for retirement with penalties for early withdrawals.  

---

## Employer-Sponsored Accounts (401k, 403b)
- Common through U.S. employers.  
- Contributions taken directly from your paycheck.  
- Often include **employer matching** (free money—always take it if available).  
- Tax treatment:  
  - Traditional 401k → Contributions reduce taxable income now, taxed later.  
  - Roth 401k → Contributions are taxed now, withdrawals are tax-free later.  

---

## Individual Retirement Accounts (IRAs)
- Available to anyone with earned income.  
- Types:  
  - **Traditional IRA** → Tax-deductible contributions, taxed when withdrawn.  
  - **Roth IRA** → Contributions after taxes, withdrawals tax-free.  
- Great for people without access to a 401k, or to supplement one.  

---

## Contribution Limits & Rules
- **401k/403b (2025 limit):** $23,000 per year, plus $7,500 “catch-up” if over age 50.  
- **IRA (2025 limit):** $7,000 per year, plus $1,000 “catch-up” if over 50.  
- Penalties apply for early withdrawals (before age 59½), with some exceptions (first home purchase, education expenses).  

---

## Quick Tips
- If your employer offers a **401k match**, prioritize contributing at least enough to get the full match.  
- A Roth IRA is ideal for younger earners who expect to be in higher tax brackets later.  
- Even if you can’t max out contributions, starting small builds lifelong habits.
