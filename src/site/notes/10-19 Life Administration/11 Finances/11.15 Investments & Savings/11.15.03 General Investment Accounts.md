General investment accounts—sometimes called brokerage accounts—let you buy and sell stocks, bonds, ETFs, and other assets outside of tax-advantaged retirement accounts. They’re flexible but don’t carry the same tax benefits.

## What is a Brokerage Account?
- An account with a financial firm (e.g., Fidelity, Vanguard, Schwab, Robinhood) that allows you to trade investments.  
- Funded with cash transfers from your bank account.  
- No contribution limits or withdrawal restrictions like retirement accounts.  

---

## Types of Investments
- **Stocks** – Ownership in individual companies, high risk/high reward.  
- **Bonds** – Loans to companies or governments, generally lower risk.  
- **ETFs (Exchange-Traded Funds)** – Baskets of stocks or bonds, easy diversification.  
- **Mutual Funds** – Professionally managed pools of investments.  
- **Other Options** – REITs (real estate investment trusts), commodities, etc.  

---

## Risks vs Rewards
- **Higher return potential** than savings accounts or CDs.  
- **Risk of loss**—value can drop quickly, especially in the short term.  
- Market timing is hard—focus on long-term investing.  
- Diversification (spreading money across different assets) reduces risk.  

---

## When to Consider a Brokerage Account
- You already have an **emergency fund** in place.  
- You’re contributing regularly to **retirement accounts**.  
- You want to save or invest for goals that aren’t retirement (e.g., a house, long-term wealth building).  
- You’re comfortable with market fluctuations.  

---

## Quick Tips
- Start with **low-cost index funds or ETFs** instead of individual stocks.  
- Don’t invest money you’ll need in the next 3–5 years.  
- Compare brokerages for fees, ease of use, and research tools.  
- Remember: investments are **not FDIC insured**.
