Online and mobile banking make it easy to manage your money without visiting a branch. Most banks and credit unions now offer secure apps and websites where you can check balances, pay bills, and transfer funds.

## Core Features
Most banks provide apps and websites that include:  
- Balance checks and transaction history.  
- Mobile check deposit (taking a photo of a check).  
- Bill pay for utilities, rent, and credit cards.  
- Transfers between your accounts.  
- Zelle or similar instant payment services.  

---

## Online-Only Banks
- Examples: Ally, Discover, Chime, SoFi.  
- Advantages:  
  - No physical branches → lower fees.  
  - Higher interest rates on savings.  
  - User-friendly apps.  
- Disadvantages:  
  - No in-person service.  
  - Cash deposits may be difficult.  
  - Fewer ATMs (look for banks with a large partner ATM network).  

---

## Security Practices
- Always enable **two-factor authentication** (text or app code).  
- Use strong, unique passwords.  
- Monitor accounts for suspicious activity.  
- Avoid using public Wi-Fi when accessing banking apps.  

---

## When to Visit a Branch
Even with strong online services, there are times you may need to go in person:  
- Opening certain types of accounts (especially for internationals).  
- Handling large cash deposits or withdrawals.  
- Getting notarized documents or official checks.  

---

## Quick Tips
- Download your bank’s official app as soon as you open your account.  
- Set up **account alerts** (text/email) for low balances, deposits, or unusual activity.  
- Online-only banks can be a great option if you don’t need to handle much cash.
