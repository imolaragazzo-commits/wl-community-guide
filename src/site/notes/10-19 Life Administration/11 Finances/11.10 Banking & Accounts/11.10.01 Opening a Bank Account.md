Opening a U.S. bank account is one of the first steps to managing money after a move. It makes paying bills, receiving paychecks, and managing everyday expenses much easier than relying on cash or international accounts.

## Why You Need a Bank Account
- Allows you to set up **direct deposit** for wages or financial aid refunds.  
- Provides a safe place to store money compared to carrying cash.  
- Enables online bill pay, debit card purchases, and transfers.  
- Helps build a financial history in the U.S. (important for credit later).  

---

## What You’ll Need
Most banks will ask for:  
- **Government-issued ID** (passport, driver’s license, or state ID).  
- **Social Security Number (SSN)** or **Individual Taxpayer Identification Number (ITIN)**.  
- **Proof of address** (lease, utility bill, or school enrollment paperwork).  
- For international students: some banks allow opening accounts without an SSN—requirements vary.  

---

## Types of Accounts
- **Checking Account** – everyday use: debit card, ATM, bill payments, transfers.  
- **Savings Account** – for storing money and earning interest (limited withdrawals).  
- Most people open **both** at the same time.  

---

## Student & Low-Fee Options
- Many banks and credit unions offer **student checking accounts** with:  
  - No monthly maintenance fees.  
  - No or low minimum balances.  
  - Free ATM access within the bank’s network.  
- Credit unions may offer better interest rates and lower fees than large banks.  

---

## Quick Tips
- Ask upfront about **monthly fees** and how to avoid them.  
- Choose a bank with ATMs near your home, campus, or workplace.  
- Online-only banks may offer better interest but have fewer ATM options.  
- If you’re new to the U.S., try a large national bank (Chase, Bank of America, Wells Fargo) for easier account setup.
