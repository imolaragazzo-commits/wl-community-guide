Banks and credit unions in the U.S. offer different types of accounts, each designed for specific needs. Most people start with checking and savings, but it helps to know the other options as your finances grow.

## Checking Accounts
- Designed for **everyday spending**: debit cards, ATM withdrawals, online bill pay.  
- Usually unlimited transactions per month.  
- May have monthly fees (often waived for students).  

---

## Savings Accounts
- Earns **interest** on money you don’t need daily access to.  
- Limited withdrawals per month (by federal law and bank policy).  
- Good for **emergency funds** and short-term goals.  

---

## Money Market Accounts
- Hybrid between checking and savings.  
- Often requires a **higher balance** but pays a better interest rate.  
- May allow limited debit card use or check-writing.  

---

## Certificates of Deposit (CDs)
- Fixed-term savings (e.g., 6 months, 1 year, 5 years).  
- Higher interest rates than regular savings, but money is **locked in** until maturity.  
- Penalties apply if you withdraw early.  

---

## Brokerage & Investment Accounts
- Offered through banks or investment firms (Charles Schwab, Fidelity, Vanguard).  
- Allows you to invest in stocks, bonds, ETFs, and mutual funds.  
- **Not FDIC insured** like bank accounts—value can go up or down.  

---

## Quick Tips
- Start with a **checking and savings** account combo—everything else is optional until you’re more established.  
- Avoid accounts with high fees unless the benefits (interest rates, perks) outweigh them.  
- Online banks often have the **best rates** for savings and CDs.
