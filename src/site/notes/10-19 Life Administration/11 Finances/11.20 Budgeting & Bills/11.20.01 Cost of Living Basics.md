Cost of living refers to the average expenses needed to cover basic needs like housing, food, transportation, and healthcare. Understanding it helps you plan your budget realistically, whether you’re moving to a new city or comparing job offers.

## What is Cost of Living?
- The amount of money needed to maintain a basic standard of living in a specific place.  
- Used to compare affordability between cities, states, or regions.  
- Employers sometimes offer **cost of living adjustments (COLA)** for jobs in expensive areas.  

---

## Major Expense Categories
- **Housing** – usually the largest expense (rent or mortgage).  
- **Utilities** – electricity, water, gas, internet, trash.  
- **Food** – groceries and dining out.  
- **Transportation** – car expenses, gas, insurance, public transit.  
- **Healthcare** – insurance premiums, out-of-pocket costs.  
- **Miscellaneous** – clothing, entertainment, personal care.  

---

## How Location Affects Costs
- Big cities (New York, San Francisco) = high housing and transportation costs.  
- Smaller cities/towns = more affordable housing but possibly fewer public transit options.  
- Taxes vary by state: income, sales, and property taxes all affect living expenses.

## Cost of Living Tools
- Online calculators and indexes can help compare cities:  
  - [Numbeo Cost of Living Index](https://www.numbeo.com/cost-of-living/)  
  - [MIT Living Wage Calculator](https://livingwage.mit.edu/)  
  - [NerdWallet Cost of Living Calculator](https://www.nerdwallet.com/cost-of-living-calculator)  
- Useful for evaluating job offers in different locations.  

---

## Quick Tips
- Rule of thumb: spend no more than **30% of income on housing**.  
- Compare cities before moving—salary differences may not cover higher living costs.  
- Build a sample budget with real numbers for rent, food, and transportation in your area.
