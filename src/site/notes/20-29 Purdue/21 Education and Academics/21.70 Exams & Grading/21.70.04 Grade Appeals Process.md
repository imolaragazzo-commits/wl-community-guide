If you believe a grade was assigned unfairly or in error, Purdue has a formal **grade appeals process**. It’s designed to resolve disputes first between student and instructor, then through department and university channels if needed.

## When to Consider an Appeal
You may file an appeal if you believe your grade was affected by:  
- A clerical or calculation error.  
- Grading that deviated from the syllabus or stated policy.  
- Evidence of bias, discrimination, or capricious grading.  

Disagreeing with an instructor’s academic judgment alone (e.g., “my paper deserved better”) is not grounds for appeal.  

---

## Steps in the Process
1. **Informal Resolution** – Speak with your instructor first. Many issues are resolved this way.  
2. **Departmental Appeal** – If unresolved, file a written appeal with the department head.  
3. **College Grade Appeals Committee** – Reviews the case and makes recommendations.  
4. **University Senate Grade Appeals Committee** – Final level, if the dispute remains unresolved.  

[Grade Appeals – Purdue Regulations](https://catalog.purdue.edu/)  

---

## Appeal Deadlines
- Appeals must typically be filed **within one semester** after the grade is assigned.  
- Earlier is better—delays can make gathering evidence more difficult.  
- Each college may set additional internal deadlines.  

---

## Limits of Appeals
- Committees cannot re-grade assignments. They only review **fairness and consistency** of grading.  
- If an appeal is upheld, remedies may include:  
  - Correcting a calculation error.  
  - Adjusting a final grade.  
  - Re-evaluating an assignment by a different instructor.  

---

## Quick Tips
- Gather documentation: syllabus, graded work, emails, and notes from conversations.  
- Approach your instructor respectfully—many appeals resolve without escalation.  
- Advisors can guide you on whether your case meets appeal standards.
