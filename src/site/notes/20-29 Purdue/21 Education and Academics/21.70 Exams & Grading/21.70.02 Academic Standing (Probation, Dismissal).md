Academic standing at Purdue is based on your GPA and progress toward degree completion. Falling below minimum requirements can place you on **probation** or lead to **dismissal**, but there are clear policies and steps to get back on track.

## Good Academic Standing
- You are in good standing if your **cumulative GPA is 2.0 or higher**.  
- Some majors/programs require a higher GPA to remain in that field (e.g., Nursing, Engineering).  
- Meeting **Satisfactory Academic Progress (SAP)** is also required to keep financial aid.  

[Academic Regulations – Purdue Catalog](https://catalog.purdue.edu/)  

---

## Academic Probation
- Students are placed on **probation** if their cumulative GPA falls below 2.0.  
- While on probation:  
  - You must achieve a **semester GPA of 2.0 or higher** in the next term to avoid dismissal.  
  - Advising meetings may be required before registration.  
- Probation may also apply if you fail to make satisfactory progress toward your degree.  

[Probation Rules – Purdue Registrar](https://www.purdue.edu/registrar/currentStudents/grades/)  

---

## Dismissal Policies
- If on probation and you earn a **semester GPA below 2.0**, you may be dismissed.  
- Dismissal is typically for a minimum of **one semester** (fall or spring).  
- Students may appeal dismissal decisions in extenuating circumstances.  
- International students should note: dismissal can impact **visa status**.  

---

## Returning After Dismissal
- Students must apply for **readmission** through the Office of Admissions.  
- Colleges may require evidence of academic improvement (e.g., coursework at another institution).  
- If readmitted, you’ll usually return on probation until your GPA recovers.  

[Readmission Information – Purdue Admissions](https://www.purdue.edu/admissions/)  

---

## Quick Tips
- Use probation as a **warning flag**, not a stopping point—resources like ASC tutoring can help.  
- Focus on fewer, manageable credit hours while recovering GPA.  
- Advisors are required allies here—lean on them for degree planning.
