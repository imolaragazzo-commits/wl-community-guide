First-year programs at Purdue are designed to make a large university feel smaller. Through **seminars** and **learning communities**, new students get academic support, mentorship, and built-in peer networks during their first year.

## First-Year Seminars
- Offered in many colleges (sometimes called “Intro to” or “Foundations” courses).  
- Small, discussion-focused classes that cover:  
  - Study skills and time management  
  - Introduction to your major and career options  
  - Building connections with faculty and peers  
- Some are **mandatory** depending on your college/major.  

[First-Year Programs – Purdue](https://www.purdue.edu/provost/students/fyp/)  

---

## Learning Communities
- Groups of 20–30 students who share courses, projects, and sometimes housing.  
- Themes range from **academic** (Engineering, Business, Data Science) to **interdisciplinary** (Sustainability, Leadership, Innovation).  
- Provide a ready-made support system of peers with similar interests.  
- Some include **residential components**, where participants live in the same hall.  

[Learning Communities – Purdue](https://www.purdue.edu/learningcommunities/)  

---

## Benefits of Participation
- Higher rates of academic success and retention for students in Learning Communities.  
- Stronger peer networks—students often say this is how they made their first friends at Purdue.  
- More faculty interaction early in your college career.  
- Opportunities for **research projects, industry partnerships, or study abroad** tied to your theme.  

---

## Quick Tips
- Apply early—many Learning Communities fill quickly.  
- If you’re undecided, pick a theme that aligns with general interests (e.g., Global Perspectives, Leadership).  
- Even if you don’t join a Learning Community, a first-year seminar can help you settle in academically.
