## How Advising Works at Purdue
- Each academic college runs its own advising system.  
- First-year students are usually assigned an advisor in their **home college**.  
- Some majors use **professional advising staff**, others use **faculty advisors**.  
- Advisors help with course planning, registering for classes, and connecting you to academic support services.  

[Purdue Academic Advising – Overview](https://www.purdue.edu/advisors/)  

---

## Types of Advisors
- **Professional Advisors**: Full-time staff who work with undergraduates on degree requirements and scheduling.  
- **Faculty Advisors**: Professors who advise students in upper-level or specialized majors.  
- **Exploratory Studies Advisors**: Guide students who are undecided or exploring majors.  
- **Honors College Advisors**: Support Honors students with course planning and research opportunities.  

[List of Advising Offices by College](https://www.purdue.edu/advisors/)  

---

## Meeting with Your Advisor
- New students meet advisors during **BGR and/or before their first semester**.  
- Most colleges require an advising meeting at least **once per semester**.  
- You’ll use **BoilerConnect** to schedule advising appointments.  
- Topics include:  
  - Picking classes for the next semester  
  - Degree progress checks (MyPurduePlan)  
  - CODO or adding minors  
  - Career or grad school planning support  

[BoilerConnect – Schedule an Advising Appointment](https://www.purdue.edu/boilerconnect/)  

---

## Switching Advisors or Majors
- If you change majors, you’ll be reassigned to an advisor in your new college.  
- The **CODO (Change of Degree Objective)** process requires advisor approval.  
- If your advising style isn’t a good fit, you can request a reassignment through your college’s advising office.  

[CODO Information – Purdue Registrar](https://www.purdue.edu/registrar/currentStudents/students/ChangeCurriculum.html)  

---

## Quick Tips
- Don’t wait until the last minute during **registration week**—advisor calendars fill quickly.  
- Come prepared with a draft schedule or list of questions.  
- Advisors can connect you with tutoring, study abroad, research, and internships—use them beyond just class scheduling.
