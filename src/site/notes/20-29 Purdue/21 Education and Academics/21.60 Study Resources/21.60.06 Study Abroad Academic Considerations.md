## Planning Ahead
- Meet with your **academic advisor** as early as freshman year if you’re considering study abroad.  
- Identify semesters where you can go abroad without delaying graduation.  
- Some majors (like Engineering or Pharmacy) require more advance planning due to rigid course sequences.  

[Study Abroad at Purdue](https://www.purdue.edu/IPPU/SA/)  

---

## Credit Transfer & Equivalencies
- Courses abroad can count as **major requirements, electives, or core curriculum** credits.  
- Purdue maintains a database of pre-approved courses at partner universities.  
- If a course isn’t pre-approved, you can submit a syllabus for review before departure.  
- Always keep copies of syllabi and coursework for smooth transfer upon return.  

[Course Equivalency Database – Purdue](https://www.purdue.edu/IPPU/SA/academics/equivalency.php)  

---

## Impact on Graduation Timeline
- With careful planning, most students graduate on time even if they study abroad.  
- Summer and short-term programs are good options if a semester abroad won’t fit your schedule.  
- Some scholarships and financial aid apply to study abroad, but check eligibility early.  

[Study Abroad Costs & Funding](https://www.purdue.edu/IPPU/SA/Costs/)  

---

## Programs & Partnerships
- Purdue partners with universities worldwide through exchange and direct-enroll programs.  
- Options include:  
  - **Semester-long programs**  
  - **Summer/short-term programs**  
  - **Faculty-led trips**  
- Specialized programs exist for disciplines like Agriculture, Engineering, and Business.  

[Programs – Purdue Study Abroad](https://www.purdue.edu/IPPU/SA/Programs/)  

---

## Quick Tips
- Start planning at least **a year in advance**.  
- Balance required major courses with electives while abroad.  
- Some programs are taught in English, even in non-English-speaking countries.  
- Apply for scholarships specific to study abroad—they often go unclaimed.
