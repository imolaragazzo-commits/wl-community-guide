At Purdue, popular classes often fill quickly. If a class is full, you may have the option to **join a waitlist** or request a **course override**. Both require careful timing and attention to instructions.

## How Waitlists Work
- Many Purdue courses use **automated waitlists** through myPurdue.  
- If a spot opens, students are notified by email in order of their waitlist position.  
- You typically have **24 hours** to register once notified.  
- Not all courses use waitlists—some majors rely on overrides instead.  

[Waitlist Instructions – Purdue Registrar](https://www.purdue.edu/registrar/currentStudents/registeringCourses/)  

---

## Joining a Waitlist
- In [myPurdue](https://mypurdue.purdue.edu/), search for your desired course.  
- If it’s full and offers a waitlist, select **“Waitlist”** when adding the class.  
- Monitor your Purdue email daily—this is how you’ll know if a spot opens.  
- If you don’t act within the time window, the seat goes to the next student.  

---

## Course Overrides
- Overrides are special permissions to enroll in a class despite restrictions.  
- Situations where overrides may apply:  
  - Pre-requisite not met (with advisor/instructor approval).  
  - Time conflict with another class.  
  - Instructor consent required.  
- Overrides are requested through the **academic department**, not myPurdue. Each department has its own process (usually an online form or email request).  

[Course Override Contacts – Purdue Registrar](https://www.purdue.edu/registrar/currentStudents/registeringCourses/)  

---

## Tips for Getting into a Full Class
- Register **as soon as your time ticket opens** to avoid waitlists.  
- Join the waitlist but also build a **backup schedule** in case a seat doesn’t open.  
- Email the instructor or advising office if the course is critical for your major.  
- Some departments add extra seats close to the start of the semester—keep checking.  

---

## Quick Tips
- Don’t rely on waitlists for graduation requirements—always have a backup.  
- Overrides aren’t guaranteed; they depend on department policies and space.  
- Be polite and concise in override request emails—advisors and professors handle many each semester.
