Purdue offers selective programs that provide extra academic challenge and community. The biggest one is the **John Martinson Honors College**, but there are also learning communities and other enrichment options. These programs often require early applications and have their own review process.

## John Martinson Honors College
- Offers **small, discussion-based classes**, undergraduate research opportunities, and an interdisciplinary curriculum.  
- Focuses on leadership, global perspectives, and scholarly engagement.  
- Requires an additional **Honors College application essay** as part of your Common App submission.  
- Admission is **highly competitive** and tied to the November 1 priority deadline.  

[John Martinson Honors College – Admissions](https://honors.purdue.edu/prospective-students/admissions/index.php)  

---

## Learning Communities
- Optional programs that group students by **academic interest or theme**.  
- Examples: Women in Engineering, Data Mine, Leadership for Social Change.  
- Provide built-in peer networks and faculty connections.  
- Not as competitive as the Honors College but some fill quickly.  

[Learning Communities – Purdue](https://www.purdue.edu/learningcommunities/)  

---

## Other Selective Programs
- **Early Start & Summer Start**: options to begin coursework in the summer before fall.  
- **Study Abroad & Global Programs**: some selective tracks begin with applications before freshman year.  
- **Scholarship/leadership cohorts**: occasionally tied to specific colleges.  

[Early Start & Summer Start – Purdue](https://www.purdue.edu/summerstart/)  

---

## Quick Tips
- Apply by **November 1** if you’re considering Honors—the extra essay is required and late apps aren’t reviewed.  
- Even if you don’t join Honors, check out **Learning Communities**—they’re one of the easiest ways to make friends fast.  
- Some programs (like Data Mine) blend academics, housing, and industry projects—great for resume-building.
